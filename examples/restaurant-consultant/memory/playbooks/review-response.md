# Playbook: Review Response

When a client gets a negative public review, here's how the consultant responds (and what the operator drafts on their behalf).

## Triage decision tree

1. **Is the reviewer accurate?** If their facts are wrong, the response should politely correct without escalating. If their facts are right, the response should acknowledge.
2. **Is this an outlier or pattern?** Single grumpy review of a known happy customer base = different reply than a 4th review this week mentioning the same issue. Patterns → fix the underlying problem first; reply later.
3. **Is the owner's emotional state OK?** Never have the owner reply when angry. Sleep on it. Operator drafts, consultant edits, owner approves and posts.

## Default structure

1. **Greet by name** if visible.
2. **Thank them for the feedback**, even when wrong (especially when wrong — disarming).
3. **Acknowledge the specific complaint** in their words (don't paraphrase into corporate-speak).
4. **State what's actually true** if their facts were off — calmly, single sentence.
5. **Offer a concrete remedy** if applicable: comp, recheck, owner's direct contact.
6. **Close warm** — invite back, sign with the actual person's first name (not "the management").

## Examples (good)

> "Andrew, thanks for the heads-up about the wait on Saturday. We were short two servers because of a flu run — that's on us, and you're right that we should have explained it at the door. If you're back, ask for me and I'll comp your starters. — Maria, owner."

> "Thanks for taking the time, Liane. The dish you describe is the carbonara — we make it traditional (no cream), which we know isn't to everyone's taste, especially when other places nearby do it the cream version. We could have flagged it on the menu more clearly. I'll add a note. Hope you'll give us another shot. — Davide."

## Examples (bad — don't do)

> "We're sorry you didn't enjoy your experience." — generic, fake.
> "Our food has been consistently rated 4.7 by hundreds of guests." — defensive, makes them feel small.
> "Please contact us at info@..." — bouncing them to a black hole.
> "Karen, you clearly have no understanding of how a restaurant works." — never. Just never.

## Operator behaviour

- When reviewing this playbook in a session, **draft 2-3 alternative versions** and let the consultant pick + edit. Don't send a single take.
- Pull the venue's brand voice from `memory/clients/<slug>/voice.md` (or sample-of-their-writing) — match it.
- After consultant approves a final version, **save the actual published text** to `memory/clients/<slug>/history.md` for future reference.
- If the same complaint pattern repeats, **flag it** to the consultant separately: "это 3-й отзыв за неделю про waitstaff. Может стоит обсудить со staff manager'ом?"

## Boundaries

- Never post the response yourself. Always: draft → consultant approves → owner posts (or consultant posts on owner's behalf if that's the agreed arrangement, documented in `memory/clients/<slug>/index.md`).
- Don't propose comping > 50 EUR / equivalent without explicit consultant approval — that's a real cost the venue absorbs.
